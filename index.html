Understanding Your Home Loan

A home loan can seem complex, but it breaks down into a few simple parts. This calculator helps you see exactly how your loan works.

Principal

This is the total amount of money you borrow from the bank to buy your home.

Interest

This is the cost of borrowing the money, expressed as a percentage. You pay this to the bank over the life of the loan.

Tenure

This is the total time, in years, you have to repay the loan in full.

The Power of Prepayment: Save Lakhs!

Making small extra payments on your home loan can have a massive impact. By paying more than your required EMI, you reduce the principal amount faster. Since interest is calculated on the outstanding principal, this simple strategy can save you a significant amount of money and help you become debt-free years earlier.

Consider this example:

On a ₹50 Lakh loan for 20 years at 8.5% interest, simply paying **one extra EMI** each year could help you save over **₹7 Lakhs** in interest and pay off your loan almost **3.5 years early!** Use the prepayment options in the calculator above to see your own potential savings.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is an amortization schedule?

The table shown in the results is your amortization schedule. It provides a month-by-month breakdown of your loan repayment. You can see exactly how much of each EMI goes towards reducing the principal and how much is paid as interest, along with your remaining balance after every payment.

Can I pay off my home loan early? Are there any charges?

Yes, you can absolutely pay off your loan early. As per RBI guidelines, there are **no prepayment penalties or foreclosure charges** on floating-rate home loans. This gives you the freedom to pay extra whenever you have surplus funds without any additional cost.

What is a good interest rate for a home loan?

Home loan interest rates vary based on the bank, your credit score, and the current economic conditions. Generally, rates offered by public sector banks like SBI or Bank of Baroda are very competitive. A "good" rate is typically the lowest rate you can get based on a strong credit score (usually 750 or above). It's always wise to compare offers from multiple lenders.

After a prepayment, should I reduce my EMI or my tenure?

Most banks will automatically reduce your loan tenure while keeping the EMI the same. This is the most beneficial option as it leads to maximum interest savings. Reducing the EMI provides more monthly cash flow but results in lower overall savings. If your goal is to become debt-free faster and save the most money, keeping the EMI constant and reducing the tenure is the better financial strategy.
